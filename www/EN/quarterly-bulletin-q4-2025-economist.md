# Quarterly Bulletin — Q4 2025

## Executive Summary

Q4 2025 witnessed a moderation in headline inflation to 5.1% (y/y) alongside resilient activity in key economic sectors, notwithstanding a deterioration in global growth conditions. The Monetary Authority maintained the policy stance at 825 basis points throughout the quarter, citing persistent core inflation and heightened external uncertainty. This bulletin provides a comprehensive assessment of macro developments, financial market conditions, and policy considerations for the terminal quarter of 2025.

## Highlights

- **Headline CPI (y/y):** 5.1% (Q4 2025, provisional)
- **Core CPI (y/y):** 4.6% (excluding food and energy, above 4.5% target midpoint)
- **Real GDP (q/q, annualised):** 1.2% (Q4 2025, provisional) — down from 1.5% in Q3
- **Unemployment rate:** 6.3% (Q4 2025) — up from 6.1% in Q3
- **Repo rate (end of period):** 825 bps (maintained throughout quarter)
- **Labour force participation:** Modest improvement at margin

> Note: Figures are provisional sample data for demonstration purposes. Official release pending.

## Economic Context

Global growth deteriorated during the quarter, reflecting weaker external demand in key trading partners and tightening global financial conditions. The manufacturing PMI remains below 50 in several advanced economies. Domestically, consumption resilience was supported by wage growth momentum and labour market stability, offsetting headwinds from subdued investment intentions.

### Prices and Inflation Dynamics

The moderation in headline inflation from 5.6% (Q3 2025) to 5.1% (Q4 2025) was primarily driven by:

1. **Base effects** from energy price volatility in Q4 2024
2. **Energy price deceleration** following global crude oil repricing
3. **Food inflation moderating** to mid-single digits

However, core inflation persistence at 4.6% reflects:

- **Services price pressures:** Particularly in healthcare, education, and accommodation
- **Wage-price dynamics:** Pass-through of labour cost increases to service producers
- **Administered price adjustments:** Utilities and transportation tariffs
- **Sectoral pricing power:** Uneven across goods and services

### Labour Market Dynamics

The labour market exhibited mixed momentum:

- **Unemployment increased** from 6.1% to 6.3%, suggesting labour demand moderation
- **Labour force participation showed marginal improvement**, indicating countercyclical participation shifts
- **Wage growth persistence** maintained at elevated levels, sustaining inflation pressures
- **Sectoral divergence:** Services employment growth outpaced goods-producing sectors

The Phillips curve relationship remains evident, though with structural shifts attributed to demographic factors and labour market frictions.

## Financial Markets and Liquidity Conditions

### Money Markets

Interbank overnight rates traded tightly around the repo rate of 825 bps, reflecting well-anchored policy expectations. Corridor operations maintained smooth functioning with minimal volatility. The standing facility utilization remained low, indicating adequate market liquidity.

### Government Securities Market

Yield curve dynamics showed compression in the long end, with 10-year yields declining by approximately 40 basis points q/q. This repricing reflects:

- Improved global risk sentiment post-Q3 uncertainty
- Expectations of prolonged policy accommodation globally
- Flight-to-quality flows into emerging market sovereigns

### Credit Market Conditions

Bank credit growth moderated to 4.2% (y/y) from 4.8% in Q3 2025, reflecting weaker demand in investment categories offset by resilient consumer credit. Non-performing loan ratios remained stable at 3.1%.

## Monetary Policy Actions and Forward Guidance

The Monetary Authority maintained the policy rate at 825 basis points through Q4 2025, citing:

1. **Lagged transmission effects** from prior tightening that require assessment
2. **Core inflation persistence** above the target band
3. **External uncertainty** warranting a cautious approach
4. **Financial stability** considerations and credit conditions

### Policy Stance Assessment

The neutral stance reflects a balanced assessment:

| Factor | Assessment | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Inflation momentum | Moderating but core sticky | Slight hawkish |
| Growth dynamics | Softening, below trend | Dovish |
| Financial conditions | Tightening globally | Dovish |
| Labour market | Cooling from lows | Neutral |

### Forward Guidance

The MPC will continue to assess data in real-time, with focus on:

- Core inflation trajectory and services pricing power
- External demand indicators and commodity prices
- Domestic financial stability metrics
- Real-time employment indicators

Policy adjustments will be data-dependent and communicated with appropriate lead time.

## Risk Assessment

### Upside Risks to Inflation

1. **Supply-side shocks:** Geopolitical tensions affecting energy and food supplies
2. **Wage-price spirals:** Elevated wage growth sustaining services inflation
3. **Global demand surprise:** Potential reversal of disinflation in partner economies
4. **Currency depreciation:** Pass-through effects on import costs

### Downside Risks to Growth

1. **External demand deterioration:** Recession in key trading partners
2. **Financial conditions tightening:** Global capital outflows and rising risk premia
3. **Domestic credit contraction:** If financial conditions tighten sharply
4. **Confidence effects:** Uncertainty about policy intentions limiting investment

## Annex: Technical Notes

### Data Sources and Methodologies

- **CPI data:** National Statistics Authority, monthly releases with seasonal adjustment using X-13ARIMA-SEATS
- **GDP estimates:** Flash estimate methodology aligned with SNA 2008 standards
- **Labour data:** Quarterly Labour Force Survey with adjusted population weights
- **Money market rates:** Reference rates computed as volume-weighted daily average from interbank trades

### Seasonal Adjustment

All series except unemployment rate are seasonally adjusted. CPI adjustment reflects seasonal patterns in food and energy categories.
