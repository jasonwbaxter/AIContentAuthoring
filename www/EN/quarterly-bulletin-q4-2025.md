# Quarterly Bulletin — Q4 2025

## Executive Summary

Q4 2025 saw a moderation in headline inflation alongside resilient activity in key sectors. The Monetary Authority maintained the policy stance through the quarter, citing sticky core inflation and uncertain external conditions. This bulletin provides an overview of macro developments, financial market conditions, and policy considerations for the end of 2025.

## Highlights

- Headline CPI (y/y): 5.1% (Q4 2025, sample figure)
- Core inflation (y/y): 4.6% (excludes food and energy)
- Real GDP (q/q, annualised): 1.2% (Q4 2025, provisional)
- Unemployment rate: 6.3% (Q4 2025)
- Repo rate: 8.25% (policy rate at quarter-end)

## Economic Context

Global growth softened during the quarter, reflecting weaker external demand and tighter global financial conditions. Domestically, consumer spending remained supported by wage growth and labour market resilience, while investment was uneven across sectors.

### Prices and Inflation

Inflation moderated in Q4, driven partly by lower energy prices and base effects. Core inflation persisted above the target midpoint, sustained by services price pressures and wage pass-through.

### Labour Market

Labour market indicators remained mixed: unemployment edged up slightly, but participation showed modest improvement. Wage growth continued to exert upward pressure on core inflation.

## Financial Markets and Liquidity

Money market rates reflected the policy rate path, while government bond yields compressed slightly as risk sentiment improved. Liquidity in the interbank market remained adequate, supported by central bank operations when needed.

## Policy Actions and Outlook

The Monetary Authority kept the repo rate unchanged over the quarter to assess the lagged effects of prior tightening. Going forward, policy decisions will weigh the balance between achieving the inflation objective and supporting a sustainable recovery.

- Near-term risks: volatile external demand, supply-side price shocks
- Policy focus: monitor core inflation, ensure market functioning, and communicate clearly
